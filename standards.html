Standards

Our Standards and Method

This page sets out the rules every Archiverse-Pakistan piece follows, on every platform. It tells you what we publish, how to read it, and what to expect from us. If a piece does not meet these rules, it does not go out.

What we publish

We publish only facts that are verified and confirmed. A fact qualifies when it rests on the primary document itself (the law, gazette notification, standard, court order, official report, or the architect's own record) or on two independent sources that agree.

Every figure, date, name, title, clause number and photo caption is checked by opening its source. Search snippets, unofficial copies and open wikis are never enough on their own. Where reliable sources disagree, we give the range and name both sources. If a point cannot be confirmed, we leave it out. We do not soften it, and we do not ask readers to fill the gap.

We do not say that something does not exist, or was never done, unless an official source confirms it: an official statement, a gazette index, a reply under right-to-information law, or an authority's own published list.

How claims are labelled

Every claim in a piece carries one of three labels, so you always know what kind of statement you are reading.

  1. Verified fact. Confirmed under the rule above. It links to its source in the text or in the source list at the end.
  2. Community experience. What students, graduates, educators or practitioners have told us about their own experience. It is real and it matters, but it is not presented as a measured fact.
  3. Opinion. Our judgement, or the author's. Scores, verdicts and recommendations are always opinion.

On the website, community experience and opinion are marked in the text where they appear, and each piece closes with a short "How to read this piece" note. On social platforms, where space is short, the label sits in the caption or on the slide.

How ideas are presented

We spend at least as much attention on solutions as on problems. An idea that is still developing, such as a proposal or a model from abroad not yet tested here, is labelled plainly as "A proposal for discussion" or "An idea to consider". It is never presented as fact or as settled policy.

Each proposal states its logic in a few steps and the assumptions it rests on. It is anchored to a confirmed precedent where one exists. It names its limits and what would need testing first. It ends with a question that invites you to build on it. The facts inside a proposal still meet the verification rule above.

Sources on every piece

Every piece lists its sources. Each verified fact can be traced to a document you can open yourself. Where a source is a standard sold under copyright, we cite its title, edition and clause and do not reproduce its text.

How we treat institutions and people

We criticise systems, decisions and outcomes, not individuals. We name a person only where their documented public role is the subject.

We never publish a serious allegation about a named institution or person without a second check: a second independent source, recorded in our working notes, and the founder's explicit acknowledgement that he has seen it.

When a piece concerns an institution, we invite it to share its position or its plans, and we publish its reply alongside the piece. We tag the bodies a piece concerns with a respectful invitation to respond, never as a call-out. Private individuals are tagged only with their consent.

Corrections

We aim never to need a correction. If an error ever gets through, we correct it openly. The piece carries a visible correction note that says what we said, what is correct, and the source. Every correction is also entered in our public corrections log, with its date. We do not silently edit published facts.

Comments

Our readers are highly qualified, and their proposals are some of the most valuable material we receive. Every piece ends by asking for them. To keep the discussion useful:

  • Be respectful. Disagree with ideas, evidence and decisions, not with people.
  • No personal attacks, insults or harassment.
  • No doxxing: do not post anyone's private details, such as a phone number, home address or private documents.
  • Back factual claims with a source where you can. Mark your own experience as experience.
  • No spam, advertising or unrelated self-promotion.

Moderation. We hide or remove comments that break these rules. Repeated or serious breaches lead to a block. We do not remove a comment because it disagrees with us. Every reply posted from an Archiverse account is approved by the founder before it goes out.

Where your comment goes. Useful comments are filed by subject in our comment library. Each week we evaluate new proposals and publish the strongest as the "Top suggestions of the week". See How we rank suggestions. A comment is treated as an idea or a lead, never as a fact: anything factual in it is verified before we publish it.

Credit. We credit a contributor by name or handle only with their permission. Without it, we describe them in general terms, such as "a structural engineer from Lahore", and only with their consent.

Images

Every piece carries pictures that help explain it. Photos are freely licensed for reuse, and each is credited on the image and in the caption, with its licence. Captions describe only what the photo shows. Where a photo cannot explain a point, we draw our own diagrams, charts and timelines.

Use of AI

Research and drafting are assisted by AI tools. Every published fact is checked against its sources, and every post is approved by the founder before it goes out. We do not use AI-generated images of real buildings, places or people and present them as real.

Independence

Archiverse-Pakistan is independent and founder-led by Rana Mohsin Ali. We publish no paid content and no sponsored posts. No firm, institution or advertiser decides what we cover or what we conclude.

Raise a concern

If you believe a piece breaks one of these rules, tell us through Join. Name the piece and the point. We will review it against this page.
