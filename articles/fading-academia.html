Fading Academia

Rana Mohsin Ali · 7 April 2026 · First published on LinkedIn

Some of our oldest architecture schools were built on strong principles, with a curriculum suited to their time, guided by legendary teachers, and shaped by alumni who became true masters of the field. Today, these institutions face growing pressure from newer, commercially driven schools that focus more on enrollment numbers than on building architectural excellence.

As professional education expanded to meet a growing population, the focus remained on creating more schools, not on keeping pace with evolving technology or future industry demands. Outdated curricula attempt to cover every aspect of architecture but often fail to build real depth in any area.

The next generation of architects will need specialization rather than generalization. Focused studies, sharpened skills, and alignment with technological advances are essential to meet the profession's evolving challenges.

Fresh graduates often begin their careers learning through trial and error, with early opportunities shaping their growth. Delays, rework, and steep learning curves are common, and most firms cannot afford to absorb these gaps on fast-moving projects.
